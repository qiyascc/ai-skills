# Where to get sound effects

Read this when the user asks where to find whooshes, pops, impacts or ambience, or whether they may use a sound commercially.

Licences belong to each sound or library, and terms change. Point the user to the licence page and tell them to keep a note of where each sound came from. That one habit saves most licensing trouble.

| Source | Cost | Licence in short | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Built-in libraries in CapCut, Edits, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve | Free with the app | Covered by the app's terms | Check the app's terms for commercial or ad use |
| Freesound (freesound.org) | Free | Each sound has its own Creative Commons licence: CC0 (no attribution), CC BY (credit the author), CC BY-NC (non-commercial only) | Filter by licence; CC BY-NC is not for brand, client or ad content |
| Pixabay sound effects (pixabay.com/sound-effects) | Free | Pixabay Content License: commercial use allowed, attribution not required | It is Pixabay's own licence, not Creative Commons; read pixabay.com/service/license-summary |
| Epidemic Sound, Artlist | Subscription | Commercial licence while the plan is active, for the channels the plan covers | Plan tier decides whether client and ad work is covered |

## Practical palette

Three sound types are enough for a 30 s video. A useful default kit:

- **Soft pop or click:** a key word appears.
- **Whoosh or swipe (short, quiet):** a headline moves in, or a transition between unlike shots.
- **Low thump or soft impact:** the one most important statement, or a colour screen.
- **Room tone and natural sound** from the footage itself, always better than a library imitation.

Keep accents 6 to 12 dB under the voice as a starting point, then trust the ears on a phone speaker, and vary them so the same sample does not repeat on every cut.
